Mindset

The operating system Cowork should become

Cowork acts as the Chief of Staff when it can understand context, watch for commitments and risk, then act by preparing briefs, drafts, and recommendations. The Executive stays in control of judgment calls and external commitments.

Step 1

Writing the Chief of Staff Constitution

The constitution is the system prompt for the Executive's operating system. It tells Cowork what to optimize for, how to make tradeoffs, when to be candid, and where it must stop and ask for approval. Without this, Cowork will summarize activity. With it, Cowork can recommend action.

Where to go in Cowork: open Cowork, create a new chat, name it Chief of Staff OS, paste the prompt below, and ask Cowork to confirm how it will operate.

            Step 3
            
Install the first operating rhythm

            
The first meeting should end with a repeatable loop, not just good prompts. The prompts below help Cowork design the recurring work, but they do not automatically turn on the automation. After Cowork returns the recurring prompt and schedule, create the automation manually in Cowork.

            
Where to go in Cowork: first use the Chief of Staff OS thread to generate the automation prompt. Then go to Cowork's Automations or recurring workflow area, create a new automation, paste in the approved recurring prompt, set the schedule, and enable it.

Nothing is sent until the Executive reviews the draft and presses Send.

            
Where to go in Cowork: start a new Cowork task named COS Email and Teams Dashboard so the build and its automations stay together. Paste Prompt 4A. Cowork builds the dashboard as an App Builder app and returns a link to open it.

            Part 2 · Step 5
            
Screen every email into the dashboard

            
Email screening is the first watch loop. Every new email gets one decision: add it to the dashboard with a draft, or skip it with a reason. Customer email gets its own Outlook folder and its own automation, so nothing from a customer is missed or quietly marked as read.

            
Where to go in Cowork: stay in the COS Email and Teams Dashboard task. Paste Prompt 5A, review the rules Cowork proposes, and approve the automation. Then do the same with Prompt 5B.

          


          

            

              
Add to the dashboard

              
Direct asks, commitments, customer or executive issues, deadlines, decisions and risks. Each gets a priority, a summary, why it matters and a draft.

            

            

              
Skip and mark read

              
Marketing, newsletters, broad announcements, automated notices and thank-you replies that ask nothing of the Executive.

            

            

              
Skip but leave unread

              
Anything from a customer, anything personal, and anything with an action still open, so the Executive still sees it.

            

          


          

            

              Automation
              
Prompt 5A: Screen every new email

              
Why it matters: the inbox is triaged the moment mail arrives, and every decision is logged so the Executive can audit it.

            

            

            

              
              
            

            

              
After Cowork answers: turn it on

              
Open Cowork Automations and confirm COS Comms Dashboard — Email screening is active. Editing an automation pauses it, so re-activate it after every change.

            

          


          

            

              Customer coverage
              
Prompt 5B: Never miss a customer email

              
Why it matters: customer work comes first in the constitution, so every customer email reaches the dashboard and stays unread until the Executive sees it.

Approve the Outlook rule when Cowork shows it, or create it yourself in Outlook under Settings › Mail › Rules: move mail from your customer domains to Inbox › Important. Then confirm COS Comms Dashboard — Customer email screening (Important folder) is active.

            

          

        


        

          

            Part 2 · Step 6
            
Watch Teams: key-person alerts and a scheduled sweep

            
Email automations don't see Teams. Two loops cover it. Instant alerts handle the few people whose messages can't wait. A scheduled sweep of every Teams chat catches everything else, including every customer message.

            
Where to go in Cowork: stay in the same task. Paste Prompt 6A, and Cowork creates one automation per key person. Then paste Prompt 6B to schedule the sweep.

          


          

            

              
Instant alerts

              
One automation per key person. Every message in that 1:1 chat is captured on the dashboard right away, with a private alert in the Executive's own Teams self-chat.

            

            

              
Scheduled sweep

              
Runs across every chat, but not channels. Each run picks up where the last one ended and captures every customer message.

            

          


          

            

              Automation
              
Prompt 6A: Instant alerts for key people

              
Why it matters: a message from a manager or a top executive should never wait for the next sweep.

Confirm there is one active automation per person, for example COS key chat alert — [Name] (1:1). The next message from one of them is your test: it should appear on the dashboard and in your self-chat.

            

          


          

            

              Schedule
              
Prompt 6B: Sweep every Teams chat on a schedule

              
Why it matters: group, meeting and customer chats move fast, and the sweep makes sure none of them slips through.

            Part 2 · Step 7
            
Make every draft send-ready

            
A draft is only useful if it's right. Cowork researches every question before it writes, answers point by point and uses placeholders instead of guessing. Its notes and sources are visible only to the Executive. A signature line tells recipients that a Cowork Chief of Staff helped.

            
Where to go in Cowork: stay in the same task. Paste Prompt 7A and Prompt 7B, and have Cowork apply both to every automation from Steps 5 and 6. Re-activate each automation after it's updated.

          


          

            

              
Every draft must

              
    
                
  • ✓Answer each question, point by point.
    • 
                
  • ✓Use a placeholder such as [CONFIRM: date] instead of guessing.
    • 
                
  • ✓Make no commitments for the Executive.
    • 
                
  • ✓Go to the right audience, with external recipients flagged.
    • 
                
  • ✓End with the signature line.
    • 
              

            

            

              
What stays private

              
Research notes and sources are saved in their own field on each dashboard item and never go into the reply. Replies to external recipients cite public sources only, never internal documents, chats or links.

            Part 2 · Step 8
            
Make it trustworthy

            
An always-on Chief of Staff earns trust by proving it ran, never stalling and never sending on its own. These prompts add a visible heartbeat, remove the approval prompts that stall background runs, and audit the whole system.

            
Where to go in Cowork: stay in the same task. Paste each prompt, then re-activate any automation Cowork edited.

          


          

            

              Heartbeat
              
Prompt 8A: Refresh the dashboard on every run

              
Why it matters: a visible Last refreshed time proves the system is alive, even on quiet days.

            

            

            

              
              
            

          


          

            

              No stalls
              
Prompt 8B: Let background runs finish on their own

              
Why it matters: a run that waits for an approval nobody sees can leave the dashboard stale for hours.

            

            

            

              
              
            

          


          

            

              Audit
              
Prompt 8C: Audit the whole system

              
Why it matters: a regular audit catches wrong skips, missed customer messages and weak drafts before they cost trust.

            

            

            

              
              
            

          


          

            

              Lessons from the live build
              
Know these before you demo

              
    
                
  • !Editing pauses automations. After any change, re-activate the automation in Cowork Automations.
    • 
                
  • !Some external replies need Outlook. Your organization may block apps from emailing people outside the company. If the dashboard's Send is blocked, send that reply from Outlook.
    • 
                
  • !Check the reply audience. A reply to a forwarded invite or an alert mailbox can go to the wrong person. When the dashboard flags it, reply in the Teams chat instead.
    • 
                
  • !Save files directly. One sweep that saved by downloading and re-uploading waited about 20 hours for an approval. Direct edits fixed it.
    • 
                
  • !Keep one feed. If OneDrive shows "conflicted" copies of the feed, ask Cowork to fold anything missing back into the main file.
    •

            Improve the OS
            
Hill climb from useful to indispensable

            
The goal is not to design the perfect Chief of Staff up front. The goal is to run one loop, learn what was wrong, tighten the constitution, and add the next job only when the first jobs are reliable.

            

              
Week 1: run the morning brief and two meeting briefs. Track what Cowork caught, missed, and over-prioritized.

              
Week 2: add follow-through automation for significant meetings and calibrate what counts as significant.

              
Week 3: add executive stakeholder and customer commitment tracking.

              
Week 4: add leadership update drafting and weekly risk review.

              
Week 5: build the Email & Teams Dashboard and turn on email screening (Steps 4 and 5).

              
Week 6: add key-person alerts and the Teams sweep, then research and sign every draft (Steps 6 and 7).

              
Week 7: add the heartbeat, remove approval stalls and run the first full audit (Step 8).
